System 2 over H-JEPA

Can a frozen hierarchical world model explore on its own when goals come from fixed mathematical patterns, a reservoir, and an expected-free-energy choice? Tested on FourRoom and Crafter with no reward, using the evaluation logic of Ertle, Levin & Scheutz's "Free Lunch" paper.

What we found

  • The world model plus EFE selection drives exploration. On FourRoom, choosing among imagined goals by novelty and ensemble disagreement beats acting on a single pattern-made goal. It also beats random actions by a wide margin.
  • Pattern structure did not matter. Structured seeds (Halley fractal, Lorenz, Lévy flight) were statistically indistinguishable from their shuffled versions and from Gaussian noise, on FourRoom, on Crafter, and in our rebuild of the Free Lunch maze.
  • Exploring more is not achieving more in Crafter. System 2 agents cover about 2.5× more ground and sometimes build a crafting table, which random play never does, but they unlock about the same number of achievements and die a little sooner.
  • Driving actions straight from the pattern hurts. The literal Free Lunch mapping (pattern label → arrow key) gets zero Crafter achievements, and pattern → single goal without EFE is the weakest model-based condition on FourRoom.

How it works

Pattern stream pt→Fixed reservoir rt→Readout → K macro-action plans→ Imagine with H-JEPA level 3→EFE: novelty + ensemble disagreement→H-JEPA top-down planner (System 1)→Actions

System 1 is the pretrained, frozen 3-level H-JEPA (Zhang, Terver, Rabbat, LeCun, Balestriero, 2026). Every level plans by gradient descent; we replace the goal image at the top level with a latent goal from System 2, so no goal image or reward is ever given.

System 2 drives a fixed echo-state reservoir (512 units, spectral radius 0.95) with a pattern. A readout turns each of K = 16 reservoir states into a short sequence of abstract macro-actions, and H-JEPA's own level-3 predictor imagines where each leads. That keeps every candidate goal on the model's manifold. EFE picks the candidate with the most novelty against an episodic memory and the most disagreement in a small ensemble trained online on the agent's own outcomes.

Crafter uses the FourRoom model fine-tuned on CrafterPlay expert data at the original 144 px. Crafter's movement keys feed the same 2D move inputs FourRoom learned, and its other 12 actions get new inputs.

Conclusions, in the Free Lunch frame

Free Lunch asked whether patterns from unrelated sources carry actionable intelligence for exploration. In a world-model agent, the answer from these runs is no: structure in the seed did not add exploration beyond what an unstructured seed with the same machinery achieved. Their second result points the same way: patterns helped there only alongside learning.

What did carry the effect is the System 2 loop itself: imagining many candidate futures with the world model and choosing by an expected-free-energy-like score. Seeds act as a source of variety, and any source of variety served equally well. That is a useful negative result for the proposal: the structure of the "Platonic" space is not doing the work here, while the active-inference selection is.

Two conditions would give patterns a fairer chance next: tasks where long-range structure matters (deceptive mazes, multi-step crafting chains), and a readout trained long enough for a pattern-specific mapping to form.

Caveats

  • AntMaze. The pretrained 3-level H-JEPA solved 1 of 11 goal tasks on this machine (paper: 73%), with matching package versions and pixel-identical rendering. We moved to FourRoom, where the model reproduces.
  • Free Lunch maze. Our rebuild of their first experiment (60 Halley fractals, 20 × 20, best of 4 mappings, 11 × 11 Kruskal mazes) did not reproduce structured > shuffled. The paper leaves the fractal's plot area and colouring unspecified, and those details decide the label balance. Results below.
  • Sample sizes are 6 FourRoom and 10 Crafter episodes per condition. Large effects are clear; effects under about one standard deviation would be missed.
  • Planner budget. FourRoom runs use a lighter planner than the paper (40 gradient steps, 100 samples), identical for every condition.
  • Crafter model is fine-tuned for 16k steps on 80 expert episodes and has no published reference score.
